What we raise

Three breeds most farms gave up on. We didn't.

Heritage breeds grow slower and cost more to keep. They also taste like something. These are the animals our grandparents raised before the supermarket flattened everything into one color of pink. We keep them going on pasture in Stowe, and we sell what we raise straight to the people who cook it.

01

Mulefoot Hogs

USDA-inspected heritage pork

The American mulefoot is one of the rarest hog breeds in the country. Its meat is dark, marbled, and deeply flavored, the kind of pork that holds up to slow cooking and long curing. Sold by the cut or as a larger share.

Year-round, while stock lasts
02

Navajo-Churro Lamb

Seasonal heritage lamb

One of North America's oldest sheep breeds, the Churro is lean, mild, and clean-tasting, with none of the muttony heaviness people expect from lamb. We raise them on open pasture and sell them seasonally.

Seasonal · Ask about availability
03

Oxford Lamb

Seasonal heritage lamb

Oxfords are big-framed English sheep that put on good, well-marbled meat. The roasts and chops are generous and rich. A strong choice for a holiday table or a freezer you want to fill once and cook from all winter.

Seasonal · Ask about availability
04

Breeding Stock

Quality heritage animals

Starting your own flock or herd, or adding rare bloodlines to one you already keep. We sell quality heritage breeding stock to other small farms who want to carry these breeds forward.

The farm

A small farm, on real Stowe land, doing it the slow way.

Harken Back Farm sits on Pucker Street in Stowe. We're a family farm, and these breeds are a lot of work, which is part of why so few people still raise them. Our animals live outside on pasture and grow at their own pace. That's the only way you get meat that actually tastes like the animal.

Stowe loves food that comes from close to home. We're proud to be one of the farms making real heritage meat available here, sold direct, with nothing in between you and the people who raised it.
