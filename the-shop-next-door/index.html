Started by a booth vendor who wanted a shop of her own.

The Shop Next Door opened in 2016, when a longtime antiques-booth vendor from the collective next door decided to set out her own table. Everything here is chosen by hand. If it made it onto the floor, she liked it enough to live with it herself.

Camden fills up in summer and empties out in winter. This shop stays open through both. So if you found something in July and thought about it all the way home, it's worth a call. Pieces are one of a kind, and good ones do leave.

What's on the floor

A little of everything, none of it twice.

  1. 01

    Antique & vintage furniture

    Tables, chairs, cabinets and the odd piece you didn't know you needed. Built to last, already proven.

  2. 02

    French linens

    Soft, laundered and worn in. Tablecloths, napkins and lengths of cloth with real age to them.

  3. 03

    Glassware, lighting & artwork

    Pressed glass, old lamps rewired to work, and framed pieces for a wall that needs something honest.

  4. 04

    Jewelry & books

    Small finds for the pocket and the shelf. Costume and fine pieces, plus stacks worth flipping through.

  5. 05

    Home & garden wares

    Crocks, tools, planters and the useful old things that look right indoors or out.

  6. 06

    Maine & New England made

    Earth-friendly goods made close to home, set alongside the vintage so nothing feels like a museum.

Worth the trip

Curated by one person, with an eye you can feel.

There's no algorithm doing the buying here. One person walks the estate sales and the auctions, picks what's good, and arranges it so the room reads like a home and not a warehouse. You'll find things you weren't looking for. That's the point.

  • One-of-a-kind New England pieces, chosen by hand
  • Vintage mixed with Maine-made, earth-friendly goods
  • Open year-round, not just for the summer crowd

The block

Mechanic Street is a small antiquing trip in itself.

The Shop Next Door sits right beside the Antiques @ 10 Mechanic collective, in the heart of downtown Camden. People plan an afternoon around this stretch. Park once, wander the block, and let the harbor pull you back outside when you're done.
